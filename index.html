About

I am a Lecturer in the Department of Computer Science & Engineering at Pundra University of Science & Technology. I completed my B.Sc. in Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Rajshahi in 2024, where my thesis, supervised by Professor Shamim Ahmad, developed a hybrid CNN–LSTM framework for force-invariant sEMG pattern recognition.

My research asks a single question in several settings: how do we make deep models legible and label-efficient enough to be trusted when the cost of a mistake is high? In my published work I apply explainability methods — LIME attributions and multi-task transformer architectures — to source-code vulnerability detection, a domain where a prediction is only actionable if an engineer can see the reasoning behind it. I am now carrying that question into medical imaging, where I lead a PRISMA-guided systematic review of self-supervised learning for label-efficient analysis of MRI, CT, and histopathology data.

Alongside research I teach undergraduate courses in algorithms, simulation and modeling, and web engineering, and I supervise more than ten undergraduate theses in medical imaging and machine learning. I am currently applying to PhD programmes in the United States for Fall 2027, and I am looking for advisors working on interpretability, computer vision, and health AI.

Research Interests

Interpretability & Trustworthy Machine Learning

Attribution methods and multi-task architectures that make transformer predictions auditable rather than merely accurate — studied so far in cross-language source-code vulnerability detection and CWE classification.

Computer Vision

Instance segmentation and representation learning: benchmarking YOLO architectures for fine-grained segmentation, attention-based tumour segmentation, and biosignal pattern recognition with CNN–LSTM models.

Health & Medical AI

Label-efficient learning for clinical imaging: self-supervised pretraining across MRI, CT, and histopathology, where annotation is scarce, expensive, and expert-dependent.
